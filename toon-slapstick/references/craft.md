# Craft notes: classic chase cartoons

What the 1940s-50s theatrical cat-and-mouse shorts do, reduced to rules you can key.
Sources: published animator breakdowns of those shorts, the standard animation texts
(Preston Blair, Richard Williams, the 12 principles), and writing on how the scores were built.
These are notes on technique. Do not reuse anyone's characters, designs, music or gag sequences.

## 1. The format

- 6-7 minutes in the originals; a short-form version is 20-60 seconds built the same way.
- No dialogue. Story is carried by pose, expression, timing and music. Screams, gulps and laughs only.
- One location, two or three characters, one simple want (food, sleep, quiet, a prize).
- The small one is clever and wins. The big one is persistent and suffers. Neither is evil:
  the big one has a fair reason to be annoyed, the small one is cheeky rather than cruel.
  If the audience pities the loser too much, the comedy becomes cruelty.
- Violence has no consequence. Flattened characters pop back. The reaction is the joke, not the pain.

## 2. Gag structure

A short is a chain of gags, each one a little bigger, ending on the biggest, then a calm coda.

| Beat | What happens | Length at 24 fps |
|---|---|---|
| Calm setup | The big one does something he cares about. Bouncy music. | 4-6 s |
| Intrusion | The small one appears in a funny way (a trunk like a periscope, an eye at a keyhole). | 3-4 s |
| Sneak | Tiptoe in steps: move 3 frames, hold 5. Music goes quiet and low. | 2-3 s |
| Snatch | Grab, a held look to check, then a 3-frame zip away. | 1 s |
| Slow burn | The victim notices. HOLD. One blink. | 10-14 frames |
| Take | Anticipation down 3, stretch up 2, hold 6-8, settle 10-12. Sting chord. | 1 s |
| Search and reveal | Suspicious looks left and right (snap, hold 8). The reveal is a picture gag. | 3-4 s |
| Caught | Freeze mid-chew, slow look up, sheepish grin, gulp. | 2-3 s |
| Scramble | Run on the spot 8-10 frames with dust, then gone in 5 frames. | 0.6 s |
| Chase | Pan background, legs a blur, the gap closes. One reversal (the small one's trick). | 3-4 s |
| Big hit | The chaser meets something solid. Silence a few frames before, then the loudest sound. | hold 10-14 |
| Topper | A second, smaller gag right after the big one, while the audience is still laughing. | 1-2 s |
| Coda | Make-up or one last twist. Sweet music. Iris out on the pair. | 3-4 s |

Rules of three: sneak in three steps, sniff twice then act, look left, look right, then the reveal.
Escalate: each gag should be bigger or faster than the one before.

## 3. Timing table (frames at 24 fps)

| Action | Frames | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Blink | close 2, hold 1, open 2 | Use one blink inside a held pose to keep it alive |
| Eye dart | 2 | Eyes move first, head follows 1-2 frames later |
| Head turn | 3-4 | Overshoot, then settle |
| Anticipation (small) | 3-4 | Opposite direction to the action |
| Anticipation (big) | 6-10 | The bigger the action, the longer the wind-up |
| Snap to a pose | 2-5 | Nearly there by frame 2 (`'snap'`) |
| Hold on a pose | 6-16 | Never hold perfectly still: bob, blink or settle |
| Take | 3 down, 2 up, hold 6-8, settle 10-12 | Eyes pop, glasses or hat fly up |
| Delayed reaction | 8-12 held | Before a take, and before falling in mid-air |
| Walk step | 8 per step | Heavy character 10-12, sneak 8-12 with holds |
| Run step | 3-4 per step | Faster than that, legs become a wheel blur |
| Scramble on the spot | 8-10 | Lean back, wheel legs, dust puffs |
| Zip off screen | 3-5 | Smear, leave speed lines and a dust cloud |
| Thrown object in the air | 10-20 | Slow at the top, fast at both ends |
| Impact | 1 frame flash, shake 8-12 | Squash the character flat on the hit frame |
| Hold after impact | 10-14 | Vibrate 5-6 frames, then stillness |
| Peel off / slide down | 10-12 | Slow, with a falling slide whistle |
| Squash on landing | 1 frame squash, 8-10 settle | Elastic settle |
| Foot tap (impatient) | 10 per tap | 3 up, 2 down, 5 rest |

Spacing: almost nothing moves at constant speed. Slow out of a pose, fast through the middle,
overshoot, settle. Constant speed is for pans and for objects already in flight sideways.

Ones and twos: characters are drawn every second frame (twos) for normal acting, every frame (ones)
for fast action and whenever the camera pans. The engine switches automatically.

## 4. Drawing and staging

- Squash and stretch keep the volume: taller means thinner. Use it on every landing, take-off, take and hit.
- Smears replace in-betweens on fast moves: stretch the character along the path for 1-2 frames.
- Line of action: lean the whole body along one curve. Lean into runs, lean back in skids and scrambles.
- Silhouette: every key pose should read as a black shape. Keep hands and props clear of the body.
- Staging is side-on like a stage, at the small character's eye level, so chases run across the frame.
- Leave room in front of a running character. Cut or pan before they hit the frame edge.
- Backgrounds are soft, painted, lower contrast, with no outlines. Characters have ink lines and flat
  colour, so they separate from the background without any effort.
- The chase background is a long strip that pans; furniture can repeat.
- Push in (zoom 1.3-1.4) for reactions; go wide for geography, chases and hits.

## 5. Sound and music

- The score is written to the action. Timing is planned on a beat grid first: at 24 fps a beat every
  12 frames is 120 bpm, every 8 frames is 180 bpm. Start moves on beats, land hits on beats.
- "Mickey-mousing": pizzicato or bassoon for sneaking steps, xylophone for tiptoes and blinks,
  a rising slide whistle for things going up and a falling one for coming down, a fast scale run for
  a scramble, timpani and cymbal for hits, muted brass "wah wah" for failure.
- Moods: bouncy two-beat for calm, low and sparse for sneaking, galloping sixteenth notes for the chase,
  warm melody for the ending.
- Silence is an instrument. Cut the music 4-12 frames before the biggest hit and during slow burns.
- Sound effects are exaggerated and musical, not realistic: boing, bonk, zip, pop, gulp, crash with
  rattling crockery after it.

## 6. Checklist before rendering

- Does every action have an anticipation and every hit a reaction?
- Is there a held pose before each big moment, and after each hit?
- Are the fast moves 5 frames or fewer?
- Does each key pose read in the contact sheet at thumbnail size?
- Is anything important hidden behind something else or off-screen?
- Does a sound land on every step, rise, fall and hit?
- Is the last hit the biggest, and is there a topper?
