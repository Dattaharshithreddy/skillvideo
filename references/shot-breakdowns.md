## 3. True story (food outbreak): 27 s, look A/B
**Arc:** knife cut carries invisible danger → person eats → source at the farm → the fatal process mistake → people get sick → death toll (somber hard stop).
- Bacteria drawn as **glowing green rod-shaped microbes** in macro shots of the rind and on a brushed-steel knife edge; the same glowing particles are tracked from shot to shot so the viewer follows the "invisible" culprit.
- Process shot: a washing machine with tanks labelled with simple chemical symbols (the only on-screen text besides captions).
- Characters: casual, friendly 3D people in a blue kitchen; sick acting (sweat, holding stomach, pale).
- Final shot: graveyard at night, cool desaturated. The tone shifts for the toll number and the video ends there.

## 4. Science experiment (microwave grapes): 28 s, look C
**Arc:** try-this hook → surprised scientist → property of the object → mechanism at macro → molecular scale → visible result simile.
- Single palette for the whole video: white + lab blue.
- Shots: fingers placing grapes → microwave → scientist's close-up reaction (grey hair, glasses, lab coat) → grape held up to light (translucent, veins visible) → glowing energy inside the grape → two grapes on the turntable with a spark between them → field arrows in 3D space → molecules (labelled spheres) losing electrons → plasma ball rising.
- Detail: translucent flesh with internal veins, wet sheen; bokeh particles at molecular scale.

## 5. Abstract concept (chaos theory): 47 s, look C
**Arc:** physical demo → many tiny differences → divergence shown visually → numbers → real-world metaphor payoff.
- A minimal pale-blue grid "lab" world with glossy purple/blue double pendulums; **motion trails** trace each path and build into a dense pattern; 3D numeric overlays (an angle readout) appear in-scene.
- The last ~10 s jump to the real world (butterfly → country sign → tornado over a farmhouse) for the famous metaphor.
- Longer than usual (47 s) because the concept needs build-up. Fine for explainers.

## Patterns to copy
1. **The first frame is mid-action.** Never an establishing shot.
2. **Every 3–5 spoken words get a new framing** (cut or whip).
3. **Make the invisible visible** (bacteria, energy, forces, blood flow) with glowing, colour-coded elements that persist across shots.
4. **Scale ladder:** person → object → macro → micro/molecule → back to person.
5. **Follow-cam on the moving object** (helmet, wrench, heart, blood cell) carries the viewer between scenes.
6. **Physical proof of fidelity:** real-world props are accurate and worn (scuffs, labels, stitching).
7. **Endings:** a comic gag, a survival, or a somber number, then an immediate stop.
