# Script formulas

**Target: 80–110 words, read in 27–37 s (~2.9 words/s).** Written as 3–6 long, flowing sentences joined with connectors, *not* choppy one-liners. The pace comes from the cuts, not from short sentences.

## Openers (the first words are the hook)
- **What-if question:** "If [strange swap/experiment] … could [cool benefit]?"
- **Try-it framing:** "If you [do odd thing], you might be surprised by what happens."
- **In medias res (story, past tense):** "His [object] was [sucked/pulled/thrown] into [danger] before it started [threatening him], too." The danger is already happening in the first sentence, with no setup.
- **Named oddity:** "This [animal/object] can [impossible-sounding ability]…"
- **"The [person] who …"** for human-interest stories: the title states the hook, the narration starts at the key moment.

## Connector words that carry the flow
`Well,` · `You see,` · `Then,` · `But as` · `which means` · `meaning` · `just` · `until` · `For this to actually work,` · `but this would probably`

## Structures
**What-if (question → reality check → twist)**
1. Pose the question with its promised upside.
2. "Well, within moments…" Explain why it fails, with a mechanism cutaway.
3. "For this to actually work, you would need…" An absurd requirement, the visual gag.
4. "…meaning you could [upside], but this would probably [downside] and you'd have to [funny consequence]." Hard stop.

**True story (past tense, chronological, 5–7 beats)**
Danger already in progress → it escalates (two concrete details) → worst moment → a lucky physical detail saves them ("…moved in just the right way to…") → final clause of escape. End on the survival, no moral.

**Science experiment**
"If you do X, you might be surprised" → "You see, [property]" → "Then, when [trigger], [mechanism step 1] until [step 2], turning it into [visual result] that [vivid simile]." Usually one long chain sentence.

**How-to / life hack**
Problem in one clause → "all you need is …" → steps as one chained sentence → result shown → tiny bonus fact.

## Rules
- Concrete numbers ("almost 20 times a second", "33 people") make it feel real, so verify every number with a web search.
- Stories use past tense; what-ifs use "would/could"; science uses present tense.
- No "in this video", no question to the viewer at the end, no call to action.
- Split the finished script into **shot fragments of 3–6 words** for `shots.json`. Break wherever the picture should change, even mid-sentence.

## Worked example (original, what-if)
> If you swapped your eyes for an eagle's, could you spot a rabbit from a mile away? Well, you could, but first your skull would need bigger eye sockets, because an eagle's eyes are huge for its head and barely move, meaning you'd have to turn your whole head to look at anything, and with so many cones packed into the back of each eye, colours would look so intense that a normal sunset might feel almost too bright to watch.

Fragments → `If you swapped your eyes` / `for an eagle's,` / `could you spot a rabbit` / `from a mile away?` / `Well, you could,` / `but first your skull` / `would need bigger eye sockets,` / …
