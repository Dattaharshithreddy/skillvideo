# Style guide: the format's DNA

Based on frame-by-frame study of 6 of the channel's recent Shorts (what-if, true story x2, science, abstract concept; 27–47 s, 1080x1920). Measured numbers and per-shot tables: `shot-breakdowns.md`. Detail standard: `detail-rubric.md`.

## Core rules
1. **Length 27–37 s** (up to ~47 s for concepts that need build-up). No intro, no logo, no outro. The first frame is already the story, and the video stops on the last word or a visual gag.
2. **A new framing every ~1.7 s on average** (range 0.6–2.5 s, one ~4 s hold allowed). Cuts land mid-sentence, roughly every 3–5 spoken words. About half the "cuts" are fast 0.3 s whip/arc camera moves within the same scene.
3. **One continuous narration take.** Calm, friendly, slightly fast (~2.8–3 words/s) conversational male voice, flowing sentences, never shouty. Light background music sits underneath.
4. **Captions:** 1–3 words at a time, exactly as spoken (lowercase unless the word is capitalised), bold rounded white font with a soft dark shadow, centred around 78% of the frame height. No colour highlight, no all-caps, no emoji.
5. **The camera is always moving:** slow push-ins, drifting orbits, follow shots, speed-ramped motion blur on impacts.

## Three looks (pick one per video and keep it consistent)
**A. Bright explainer (what-if, body, "how X works")**
- Saturated, glossy, slightly game-like realistic 3D, like high-end medical illustration mixed with a video game.
- Plain, softly lit studio backdrops: pastel lavender, sky blue or warm yellow. Or a simple bright location (athletics track, kitchen, classroom).
- Anatomy rendered like a medical atlas: glossy red muscles, blue/red vessels, translucent bones.
- X-ray mode: dark navy background with glowing cyan fresnel-edged body outlines, and the one important organ in full colour.

**B. Realistic story ("He was…", "The woman who…")**
- Real setting rendered realistically (carrier deck at night, farm, hospital, street), with naturalistic lighting and some dusk/night scenes.
- Period/occupation-accurate costume (flight-deck jumpsuit + helmet, etc.).
- Drama through motion blur, debris, smoke and speed ramps, never gore.

**C. Science / experiment ("If you … you might be surprised")**
- One clean colour palette for the whole video (e.g. white + lab blue).
- Starts with a person doing the thing (hands in frame, a scientist's reaction close-up), then dives to macro and molecular scale: glowing particles, labelled molecules, field-line arrows drawn in 3D space.

## Signature visual devices
- **Glowing invisible agents:** bacteria, energy, forces drawn as colour-coded glowing particles that persist from shot to shot.
- **Follow-cam:** the camera chases a moving object (heart, wrench, helmet) from one setting into the next.
- **Motion trails / 3D readouts:** for abstract ideas, trails draw paths, and numbers (angles, temperatures) float in 3D space.
- **Porthole cutaway:** a clean circular or rectangular hole in the shirt/skin/jeans/machine wall showing the inside (a chest full of hearts, leg muscles under torn denim). This is the most-used trick.
- **Gloved hands + tools:** blue nitrile gloves with forceps/tweezers placing, removing or holding things. Great for transplant/"what if you swapped X" ideas.
- **Transparent machine:** see-through engine, pipe or building with the subject visible inside.
- **Scale jump:** person → organ → cell → molecule within 3–4 shots.
- **Recurring everyman:** one simple, likeable character (plain coloured t-shirt, jeans, distinctive head/hair) reused across many shots and even videos. Create *your own* mascot; don't copy the channel's.
- **Endings:** a comic gag (stuffing his face with mountains of food), a survival, or a somber number (a night graveyard for a death toll), then an immediate stop.

## Shot rhythm template (30 s)
| Time | Shot purpose |
|---|---|
| 0–2 s | Hook visual already mid-action (forceps pulling a heart out of a bird; wrench flying into an engine) |
| 2–8 s | Set up the premise, 3–4 shots, character appears |
| 8–20 s | Mechanism: cutaways, x-ray, macro, 6–8 shots |
| 20–28 s | Consequence/escalation, back to the character |
| last 2 s | Payoff or gag, then hard stop |

## Don't
- Dark, moody "cinematic" grading for explainers. Explainers are bright and clean.
- Text baked into images, except simple in-world labels (chemical symbols on tanks, a 3D angle readout, a road sign).
- Real people, brand logos, copyrighted characters, gore.
