## A. Verified facts

**The twelve principles.** Introduced by Disney animators Ollie Johnston and Frank Thomas in their 1981 book
*The Illusion of Life: Disney Animation*; they grew out of the studio's work from the 1930s on. They warn against "twins",
where a character's left and right sides mirror each other. [verified 2026-11-11]
Sources: https://www.bloopanimation.com/the-12-principles-of-animation/ ·
https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/animation/discover/principles-of-animation.html ·
https://www.netguru.com/blog/illustrators-eye-12-principles-of-animation

**Classic theatrical cat-and-mouse shorts (our reference for timing and physical comedy).**
William Hanna and Joseph Barbera made 114 Tom and Jerry shorts for MGM between 1940 and 1958; seven won the Academy Award
for animated short. Each runs about 6 to 10 minutes. Scott Bradley wrote the music. The characters rarely spoke, and Hanna
supplied most of their vocal effects. MGM closed the cartoon studio in 1957. [verified 2026-11-11]
Sources: https://firstversions.com/2019/12/tom-and-jerry-cartoon.html · https://www.tbsnews.net/node/46609 ·
https://televisionheaven.co.uk/reviews/the-tom-jerry-show
*What we take from them:* silent storytelling, music carrying the emotion, vocal effects instead of dialogue. *What we do not take:* characters, designs, gag sequences, music.

**Scenes that show a cat drinking milk** (for staging study): *The Milky Waif* (1946), *Dr. Jekyll and Mr. Mouse* (1947). [verified 2026-11-11]
Sources: https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/WesternAnimation/TheMilkyWaif · https://heykidscomics.fandom.com/wiki/Dr._Jekyll_and_Mr._Mouse

## B. Our own measurements [measured, November 2026]

From stepping through a classic chase cartoon on YouTube frame by frame: 24 fps source; shots have a median length of
about 4.3 s; about 14% of frames are completely still; the fastest moves last 1-2 frames. Details in
`toon-slapstick/references/craft.md`.

## C. Books to work from [established; not re-read today, cite page numbers only after checking a copy]

| Priority | Book | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Richard Williams, *The Animator's Survival Kit* | Walks, runs, spacing charts, timing, ones and twos |
| 2 | Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, *The Illusion of Life* | The principles, acting, staging |
| 3 | Preston Blair, *Cartoon Animation* | Construction of cartoon characters, takes, cycles, by an MGM-era animator |
| 4 | Eric Goldberg, *Character Animation Crash Course!* | Breakdowns, overlap, comedy timing |
| 5 | Harold Whitaker and John Halas, *Timing for Animation* | Frame counts for weight and force |
| 6 | Eadweard Muybridge, *Animals in Motion* | Real animal locomotion, frame by frame |
| 7 | Walt Stanchfield, *Drawn to Life* | Gesture and line of action |
