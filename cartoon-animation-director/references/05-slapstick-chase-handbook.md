# Slapstick comedy and chase handbook

Tags: **[established]** standard teaching · **[measured]** from our own frame study of classic chase cartoons · **[recommendation]** our rule.
Everything we make is original: no existing studio's characters, designs, specific gag sequences or music are copied.

## 1. The shape of a gag

| Beat | What happens | Typical length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Setup | We see the place, the object and that all is calm. | 1.5-3 s |
| 2. Intention | A character wants something. Show it in the eyes first. | 0.5-1 s |
| 3. Anticipation | They prepare. The audience now expects an outcome. | 0.3-1 s |
| 4. The unexpected | Something else happens instead. | 2-6 frames |
| 5. Impact or surprise | One clear frame that shows what went wrong. | 1 frame plus a 4-8 frame hold |
| 6. Reaction | The victim understands. This is where the laugh is. | 0.5-1.5 s |
| 7. Recovery or reversal | They try again, or the tables turn. | 1-2 s |
| 8. Complication | It gets worse, bigger, faster. | repeats 2-7 |
| 9. Ending | Pay-off, then a small topper, then a warm final pose. | 2-4 s |

**Why the reaction matters most [established]:** the audience laughs at the character realising, not at the event. Cutting
away or moving on before the reaction is held kills the joke.

## 2. Timing tools

- **The pause before the hit.** Stop the music and hold still for 4-12 frames just before the biggest impact.
- **The slow burn.** After being hit, the character does nothing for half a second, then slowly turns to look at whoever did it.
- **The delayed reaction.** The character carries on for a beat before noticing (walks off a ledge, keeps walking, looks down).
- **The take.** Blink, squash down, stretch up with eyes wide, hold, settle. Bigger surprise, bigger take.
- **The look to camera.** One flat look at the audience, 8-12 frames. Use once per episode.
- **Rule of three.** Do it, do it bigger, then break the pattern. Never four.
- **Speed contrast [measured].** Fast moves take 1-5 frames, holds 8-20. Mid-speed everything is what makes animation mushy.
- **Silence.** Sound drops out before the hit and returns on the contact frame.

## 3. Staging comedy

- One idea on screen at a time. If two characters act at once, one of them freezes.
- The victim and the cause are both in frame for the impact; then cut or push in to the victim for the reaction.
- Keep the camera still during a gag. Move it only to follow travel or to reveal.
- Hide and reveal: let the audience see the danger before the character does (superiority), or reveal it to both at once (surprise).
- Props must be established before they matter. The banana skin is seen before anyone steps on it.

## 4. Sound in slapstick

- Every step, rise, fall and hit has a note or an effect ("mickey-mousing").
- Pitch follows height: a slide up as something rises, down as it falls.
- Our palette is Indian: tabla for steps and hits, bansuri for giggles and falls, veena for surprise, ghatam for ticks, manjira for sparkle.
- The biggest hit is the loudest sound and the only one on that frame.
- Claude cannot hear the mix. Harshith must judge it by ear.

## 5. Chase choreography

- **Screen direction.** If they run left to right, they keep running left to right until a turn is shown on screen.
- **Geography.** Show the space once (wide) so the audience knows where the door, the table and the dead end are.
- **Gap.** The distance between chaser and chased changes: closing, near miss, escape. A constant gap is dull.
- **Obstacles in threes.** Under, over, through; the third one catches somebody.
- **Reversal.** At least once the chased becomes the chaser.
- **Breather.** One still moment in the middle (both stop, pant, look at each other, go again).
- **Exit and entrance.** Leaving frame: wind-up 6-8 frames, gone in 1-3, dust hangs. Entering: arrive past the mark and settle back.
- **Background.** A long painted background that scrolls; repeat it only if the repeat is not noticeable.

## 6. Our series rules that shape the comedy [confirmed by Harshith]

- Bheemu and Gaju are friends. Gaju starts the trouble, Bheemu pays for it, and they end together.
- Less violence than classic chase cartoons: bonks, splashes and falls, never weapons or cruelty.
- Gags come from what the characters are: Gaju's trunk and ears, Bheemu's size and calm, Ammamma always knowing.
- The home is Telugu and specific: dosa, papad, mango tree, tulasi, the lane's tea stall, festivals.
- Written jokes live on the walls and signs, never in speech bubbles.

## 7. Exercises (original situations)

1. **Hiding.** Gaju hides behind something too small. Three hiding places, the third works for the wrong reason.
2. **Slipping.** Bheemu and a wet floor. Hang in the air before the fall.
3. **Dodging.** Three things are thrown; the dodge of the third puts him in the path of a fourth.
4. **Getting stuck.** A trunk in a pot. Three ways to get it out, each worse.
5. **Unexpected object.** A box that should contain one thing contains another. Reaction is the whole exercise.
6. **Chase.** Twelve seconds, one reversal, one near miss, one breather.

Each exercise is scored with `07-qc-rubric.md`, plus one question: can a first-time viewer retell the gag in one sentence?

## 8. Common mistakes

| Mistake | Detect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No setup: the gag starts immediately | First 2 seconds show no calm state | Add a calm beat |
| Reaction too short | Fewer than 12 frames on the reaction pose | Hold it; add a blink or a slow turn |
| Everything the same speed | No move under 5 frames and no hold over 8 | Re-time to fast and still |
| Too many ideas | Cannot retell it in a sentence | Cut to one object, one want |
| Topper missing | Ends on the main hit | Add one small extra beat, then the warm pose |
| Characters explain the joke | Text or speech describing what happened | Remove it; show it |
